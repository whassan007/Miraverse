# MiraVerse v1.2 — Localization & Regional Dialect Preservation Review

- **Locales Supported:** `en`, `ar-LB` (Lebanese Arabic), `ar-IQ` (Iraqi Arabic), `ar-EG` (Egyptian Arabic), `fr` (French / Lyonnais), `ko` (Korean Hangul & Revised Romanization).
- **RTL Layout Verification:** Arabic cards and encyclopedia headers apply `dir="rtl"` automatically when `ar-LB`, `ar-IQ`, or `ar-EG` is selected.
- **Historical Register Guardrail:** Ancient Phoenician (King Ahiram) and 9th-century Abbasid/10th-century Fatimid historical figures never speak modern street slang; contemporary mentors (Teta Nahla/Yasmine, Archivist Tariq, Master Hassan) use warm regional dialect greetings.

## 1. Lebanese Arabic (`ar-LB` — Owner: `DX14`)
- **تيتا** (*tēta*): grandmother (Levantine dialect; never replace with MSA جدة in dialogue)
- **منقوشة عالصاج** (*manʾūshe ʿaṣ-ṣāj*): za’atar flatbread baked on a domed iron griddle
- **حوش الحجر** (*ḥōsh el-ḥajar*): stone courtyard
- **مينا جبيل** (*mīna Jbeil*): Byblos harbor
- **أبجدية فينيقية** (*abjadiyye fīnīqiyye*): Phoenician consonantal alphabet (22 letters)
- **أرشيف متنقل** (*arshīf mutanaqqil*): portable cultural archive

## 2. Iraqi Arabic (`ar-IQ` — Owner: `DX15`)
- **شلونك / شلونچ** (*Shlonak / Shlonich* — `contemporary_baghdadi_greeting`): Contemporary Al-Mutanabbi Street & diaspora festival mentors only; never used by 9th-century Abbasid scholars.
- **بيت الحكمة** (*Bayt al-Hikma* — `classical_and_modern_standard`): House of Wisdom translation and research library in Baghdad.
- **كتاب الحيل** (*Kitab al-Hiyal* — `classical_scientific`): Book of Ingenious Devices by the Banu Musa brothers (c. 850 CE).
- **شارع المتنبي** (*Shari' al-Mutanabbi* — `contemporary_literary`): Baghdad's historic street of booksellers and outdoor Friday book tables.
- **كليچة بالتمر والهيل** (*Kleicha bil-Tamr wal-Heil* — `iraqi_culinary_heritage`): Stamped date-and-cardamom pastry wheel shared across Iraqi communities.

## 3. Egyptian Arabic (`ar-EG` — Owner: `DX15`)
- **أهلاً وسهلاً / إزيك** (*Ahlan wa Sahlan / Izzayak* — `contemporary_cairo_greeting`): Warm contemporary Cairo courtyard greeting for Master Hassan.
- **فانوس / فوانيس** (*Fanous / Fawanees* — `egyptian_heritage_craft`): Traditional tin-and-colored-glass Cairo lantern (singular/plural).
- **شارع الخيامية** (*Shari'a al-Khayamiya* — `historic_cairo_toponym`): Street of the Tentmakers at the covered Qasaba of Radwan Bey near Bab Zuweila.
- **مسحراتي / طبلة البزازة** (*Musaharati / Bazaza* — `cultural_practice`): Pre-dawn neighborhood drummer calling neighbors by name with gentle rhythm.
- **تحت الربع** (*Taht al-Rab'* — `historic_cairo_toponym`): Historic lantern-making quarter near Bab Zuweila.

## 4. French / Lyonnais (`fr` — Owner: `DX16`)
- **Traboule / Trabouler** (`fr-FR`): From Latin transambulare ('to walk through'); covered Lyon passageway connecting streets through interior courtyards.
- **Les Canuts** (`fr-FR`): Master silk weavers of the Croix-Rousse hill in 19th-century Lyon.
- **Bistanclaque-pan** (`fr-FR`): Lyonnais onomatopoeia imitating the rhythmic clatter of a wooden shuttle and Jacquard loom.
- **Cour des Voraces** (`fr-FR`): Iconic 1840 six-story courtyard staircase at 9 Place Colbert / 29 Rue Imbert-Colomès.
- **Les Mères Lyonnaises & Bouchons** (`fr-FR`): Women chef-proprietors such as Eugénie Brazier who shaped Lyon's culinary excellence.

## 5. Korean Hangul & Revised Romanization (`ko` — Owner: `DX16`)
- **전주 비빔밥** (*Jeonju Bibimbap* — `ko-KR`): Mixed rice bowl of Jeonju prepared with broth-steamed rice and seasonal namul.
- **오방색** (*Obangsaek* — `ko-KR`): Traditional Korean five-direction color spectrum: Cheong (Blue/Green), Jeok (Red), Hwang (Yellow), Baek (White), Heuk (Black).
- **시의전서 (골동반)** (*Siuijeonseo (Goldongban)* — `ko-KR`): Late-19th-century Korean cookbook containing the earliest known written hangul recipe for bibimbap.
- **전주 한옥마을 · 온돌 · 대청마루** (*Jeonju Hanok Maeul · Ondol · Daecheong Maru* — `ko-KR`): Traditional tiled-roof house village with underfloor heating and open wooden breeze hall.
- **장독대 (옹기)** (*Jangdokdae (Onggi)* — `ko-KR`): Sunlit courtyard terrace of breathable earthenware crocks for fermenting gochujang, doenjang, and ganjang.
