## 1. Child Safety & Epistemic Boundaries

### A. Non-Violent Educational Design
- **Military Siege Context:** In representing the sieges of Tyre (332 BCE) and Motya (397 BCE), the experience does not implement weapons, combat, or violent depictions. The learning focus is centered strictly on **civil engineering, bathymetric geography, and logistical problem-solving** (e.g., how building a causeway affects sediment movement).
- **Sacrificial Practices & Tophet Sensitivity:** The Punic Tophet at Carthage is treated strictly as an **epigraphic stelae gallery**. It introduces children to the archaeological debate between infant cemetery and ritual practices through age-appropriate museum stelae with Tanit symbols, with zero graphic imagery.
- **Religious Neutrality & Cultural Respect:** The aniconic Phoenician tradition (empty thrones, sacred standing baetyls) is explained descriptively as an ancient cultural worldview without forcing players to participate in religious worship.

### B. Material Culture & Ancient Craft Mechanics
- **Tyrian Purple Dye:** Explains the photolytic oxidation chemistry of 6,6'-dibromoindigo through a color-matching sorting puzzle. Players observe yellow snail secretion turn green and then deep royal purple under sunlight. Cruelty-free: no animals are harmed or killed in the game mechanic.
- **Core-Formed Glass of Sidon:** Players trace molten glass threads around an organic clay core to create decorative zigzags, learning ancient material science prior to glassblowing.
- **Phoenician Alphabet Decipherment:** Players connect the 22 linear phonetic symbols of King Ahiram's inscription (`𐤀`, `𐤁`, `𐤂`, `𐤃`) with modern Latin and Arabic consonant phonemes, understanding how writing evolved from cumbersome pictograms to phonetic sounds.
