## 1. Stratigraphic Principles & Anti-Anachronism Boundary

A fundamental rule of this historical world implementation is that **divergent historical eras must never be collapsed into a single simultaneous space without explicit stratigraphic or museum framing**. 

In historical Byblos, the Temple of the Obelisks was constructed directly over the foundations of the earlier 3rd-millennium BCE L-Shaped Temple. In 1930–1938, French archaeologist Maurice Dunand painstakingly numbered, dismantled, and moved the upper Temple of the Obelisks stone-by-stone 100 meters east to permit the excavation of the older Bronze Age layers beneath. 

In our Roblox reconstruction:
- The **Temple of the Obelisks** stands at its primary Middle Bronze Age (c. 1900–1600 BCE) elevation.
- The **Earlier L-Shaped Temple** is implemented as an adjacent excavated stratum (-2 studs depth) with boundary grid markers and educational stratigraphy comparison plaques.
- Players are explicitly taught that these two monuments represent distinct millennia of continuous sacred veneration rather than simultaneous side-by-side buildings.

## 3. Battles and Conflict Policy

Historical military conflicts (Salamis 480 BCE, Motya 397 BCE, Tyre 332 BCE, Aegates 241 BCE) are represented exclusively as **geoarchaeological and civil engineering exhibits**:
- **Tyre (332 BCE):** Modeled through coastal sediment accumulation and the physics of the tombolo isthmus.
- **Motya (397 BCE):** Modeled through shallow-water lagoon logistics, causeways, and architectural problem-solving.
- **Aegates Islands (241 BCE):** Modeled through forensic nautical archaeology of the Marsala shipwreck's mortise-and-tenon joints and painted carpenter assembly signs.
- **Child Safety Compliance:** Zero graphic depictions, zero violent reenactments, and zero military glorify mechanics.
